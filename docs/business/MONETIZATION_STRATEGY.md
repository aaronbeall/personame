# Personame monetization strategy

Decision draft, October 28, 2026. Proposed offers and numbers below are hypotheses, not implemented plans, validated willingness to pay, or revenue forecasts. Based on the [competitive analysis](COMPETITIVE_ANALYSIS.md); execution is in the [implementation plan](IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md).

## 1. Recommendation

**Priority revision, October 28:** the [product thesis](PRODUCT_THESIS.md) puts creative dimension/archetype models, model diagnostics, illustrated results, and curated collections first. Business insight is an optional expansion when demand appears. The prices and business-first sequence below are conditional commercial experiments, not the required core roadmap; advanced creator tools and optional collector cosmetics/art deserve earlier testing alongside them.

Keep participation, full core results, saving discovered selves, and voluntary sharing free. Earn revenue primarily from **creators and businesses paying to make better quiz experiences and learn from appropriately disclosed campaign participation**.

Test paid creator value after validating the core experience: clearer model design/diagnostics, coherent art-authoring tools, and premium presentation are plausible offers alongside business-oriented capabilities. Optional collector purchases can be tested without gating core results. Test audience insights through a small, separately scoped pilot if buyer demand appears, before introducing a business subscription. Keep consumer discovery and commercial analytics visibly separate. A trusted consumer relationship is not implicit permission to build a saleable profile.

The business customer is initially a newsletter publisher or community-led brand with its own distribution. They buy time saved, brand presentation, repeatable engagement, and a better-informed audience decision. They do not buy access to the private identities or unrelated quiz histories of Personame users.

## 2. Revenue sequence

| Order | Offer | Why now or later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First validation | 3–5 concierge pilots, **$300–$750 each**, fixed scope | Tests payment and a real decision before a large reporting/billing build. A pilot buys one campaign setup, agreed report, and review; distribution is the customer's responsibility. |
| First scalable offer | **Studio, $29/month** | A straightforward creator subscription for publishing capacity, presentation, funnel reporting, and a useful result CTA |
| After insight pilots renew | **Business, $99/month** | Adds governed campaign research, aggregate exports, attribution, and a small team workflow once those exist |
| Later experiment | Optional cosmetic packs/supporter purchases | Can enrich collecting selves without withholding the result or implying paid insight is more truthful |
| Only with real distribution | Clearly labeled sponsorships | Price creation and placement separately; sell reach only after it is measured |

These pilot prices do not include unlimited revisions, custom integrations, guaranteed response counts, or statistical representativeness. Record service hours and contribution separately from subscription MRR. Suggested pilot scope: one quiz, one revision round, one predeclared audience question, a report if the agreed data threshold is met, and a 45-minute review. Agree the low-response outcome/refund or extension terms before payment.

Do not start with programmatic ads, commissions on selling personal data, hidden subscriptions, a forced email gate before results, or an expensive consumer assessment report. The studied competitors already span free creation and mature business workflows; lower price alone is not a sound position. See [competitive pricing](COMPETITIVE_ANALYSIS.md#2-competitor-map).

## 3. Packaging to test

Monthly USD, before tax. Do not publish this as current product pricing. Introduce annual billing only after observing retention; start month-to-month so cancellation and willingness to renew are visible.

| Capability | Free | Studio: proposed $29/month | Business: proposed $99/month, later |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Take quizzes, view the full core result, keep/share selves | Free | Same | Same |
| Draft authoring | Generous fair use | Same | Same |
| Published quiz slots | 3 | 10 | 25 |
| Authoring | Core engine and starter templates | Duplication/templates, more presentation options, campaign organization | Studio plus reusable research setup |
| Presentation | Personame attribution | Custom logo and premium layouts; host/provider disclosure remains | Embed and brand workspace, with sponsor/host disclosure |
| Operational reporting | Total completions | View/start/completion funnel and coarse campaign-source trends | Same plus approved campaign comparisons |
| Audience insight | None | None beyond operational reporting | Opted-in answer/outcome aggregates, approved optional demographic fields, minimum-cohort controls |
| Exports/integrations | No participant-level export | Basic aggregate funnel export | Governed aggregate CSV and one demand-led integration; no automatic raw-person export |
| Team access | Individual creator | Individual creator | Up to 3 workspace members with roles |
| Included research processing | Not applicable | Not applicable | Initially 5,000 eligible insight completions/month; validate the limit in pilots |

Protect existing creators: do not automatically unpublish quizzes or erase data when introducing limits. Grandfather existing published slots; define how a temporarily edited draft retains its allocated slot. Downgrading stops new premium actions after a communicated grace period, rather than breaking participant results or silently charging overages.

Business usage counts a completed attempt included in consented campaign research, not each page view, individual answer, or retry. Repeated new attempts can count separately for processing, while reports explain their deduplication policy. Exclude previews, bots flagged by the quality policy, idempotent retries, and non-consented research. Maintain an auditable adjustment path. Do not retroactively monetize historic participation.

No automatic overage charges at launch. Warn the creator before the research allowance is exhausted; offer an explicit capacity purchase once cost is measured. Reserve capacity for already admitted research attempts. At the cap, stop inviting new research contributions while keeping the core quiz/result experience available. The disclosure must reflect whether research is actually being collected.

The Free experience is not a promise of unbounded infrastructure: use transparent anti-abuse/rate policies and operating budgets. A participant should never finish a quiz and discover their result is held for payment because a creator exceeded a plan limit.

## 4. What businesses actually learn

Sell answers to specific decisions:

- A newsletter editor compares which **self-reported topics** respondents want next.
- An event community tests interest in quieter meetups versus larger gatherings.
- A hobby brand learns which optional product interests respondents report, without inferring income or health from an archetype.

Each report states campaign/version, dates, eligible response count, opt-in count, missing-field rate, repeat-handling policy, and collection source. Show “opted-in responses,” not “your customers” or “unique people” unless the population and identity claim are established. Demographic breakdowns require separately collected optional information; do not infer age, gender, ethnicity, or income from a quiz score or OAuth profile.

Optional initial fields could be broad adult age bands, broad region, and relevant interests. Exclude exact date of birth, precise location, sensitive categories, and unrestricted custom research fields from the first pilot. Start commercial research pilots with adults; this is a product scope decision, not a statement that all jurisdictions have the same age rules. Decide consumer age policy before marketing to younger audiences.

Do not pool unrelated custom dimensions into a global “personality database.” A future cross-campaign benchmark would require deliberately standardized instruments, comparable versions, explicit permissions, qualified methodology, and sufficiently broad samples. It is not part of launch revenue.

## 5. The trust contract

1. **Name the business and purpose before the quiz.** A commercial or sponsored label must be visible on discovery cards and the quiz cover, not only after completion. [FTC native-advertising guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/native-advertising-guide-businesses)
2. **Deliver the promised result regardless of optional research or marketing choices.** After revealing it, offer a clear, unchecked choice to contribute the stated result/answers to the named campaign's research. Demographics remain skippable. Newsletter/marketing opt-in is a separate, recipient-specific choice.
3. **Do not equate sign-in with business permission.** A creator cannot use a participant's Google/GitHub email, account history, or other selves simply because they took the quiz. Contact details are a separate voluntary submission.
4. **Make sharing deliberate.** Default new results to owner-controlled access; create a separate share artifact/token when requested. Preview exactly what it exposes. Never put demographics or research answers into the shared card or metadata.
5. **Give people controls.** Access, deletion, withdrawal, share revocation, and clear retention notices apply regardless of payment tier. Use plain language and comparable accept/decline prominence. [FTC guidance on manipulative privacy and subscription design](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2022/09/how-companies-manipulate-you-online-what-ftc-doing-protect-you)
6. **Report conservatively.** Proposed pilot policy: require at least 100 opted-in responses for a segmented report, and suppress any released cell below 20 eligible participant tokens, including complementary cells. Restrict filter combinations and repeated differencing. These are proposed product thresholds, not proof of anonymity, representativeness, or a legal safe harbor. [ICO identifiability guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/anonymisation/how-do-we-ensure-anonymisation-is-effective)
7. **Never paywall certainty.** Creator-defined results are playful reflections, not diagnosis, suitability screening, probability estimates, or scientific certification.

Basic service operations and optional campaign research are different processing purposes. Establish the appropriate notices, roles, lawful basis, retention, and contracts for intended launch markets with qualified review. Do not claim “GDPR compliant,” “anonymous,” or “zero-party data” as substitutes for that work.

Existing results are not permissioned research records. Do not backfill research consent. Existing bearer-link results need a deliberate migration policy before promising private-by-default access; previously shared links must not silently become broader marketing profiles.

## 6. Distribution and the social loop

The core sequence should be: finish a good quiz → receive a satisfying archetype → optionally save/share a selected card → a friend takes that same quiz → discover another relevant quiz or make one. Keep all commercial asks subordinate to that sequence.

Prioritize image-ready cards and reliable Open Graph previews, a short caption, a quiz invitation, a non-sensitive referral token, and a post-result suggestion. Comparison is optional, mutual, and scoped to the same quiz/version. Do not expose friends' histories, scrape contacts, or infer that a clipboard copy means a real share.

Track share intent, successful handoff/copy, referral arrivals, and referred completions separately. Exclude crawlers and preview fetches. A returning collection is a retention hypothesis; test whether it produces second-quiz completion before building feeds, chat, streaks, or a full social graph.

Customer acquisition initially comes from the buyer's own audience, founder-led interviews, worked examples, and creator referrals. No paid consumer acquisition until attributable downstream customer contribution justifies it. Do not assume viral distribution subsidizes every free completion.

## 7. Economics and sustainability

Current hosting entry-cost notes are in [Deployment](../DEPLOYMENT.md). A $20–$40 initial hosting budget is not a forecast for a large free social audience.

Use this model:

`monthly contribution = subscription revenue - payment/billing fees - variable delivery cost - allocated support - fixed operating services`

Illustrative mature mix, **not a forecast**: 40 Studio accounts × $29 + 10 Business accounts × $99 = **$2,150 MRR**. Fifty monthly domestic-card subscriptions at Stripe's observed standard 2.9% + $0.30 plus 0.7% Billing cost approximately **$92.40**. International cards, tax tooling, refunds, disputes, and other services can change this. [Stripe Payments](https://stripe.com/en-us/pricing), [Stripe Billing](https://stripe.com/en-us/billing/pricing)

Assume $100 fixed operating services, 10 hours of monthly support valued at $50/hour, and **$0.005 variable delivery cost per completion** as a sensitivity input. This last figure is not a measured Personame cost or a vendor quote; it must include allocated traffic from visitors who never complete, images, compute, storage growth, and event ingestion without double-counting fixed services.

| Total free + paid completions/month | Assumed variable cost | Contribution before development, acquisition, tax, and other overhead | Contribution / revenue |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10,000 | $50 | $1,407.60 | 65.5% |
| 100,000 | $500 | $957.60 | 44.5% |
| 1,000,000 | $5,000 | −$3,542.40 | Negative |

A viral audience can lose money at a fixed paid-customer count. Measure costs per visit, start, completion, image delivery, and retained result; compare normal and burst traffic. Pre-render/cache share assets, bound analytics events, index queries, and add bot controls. Set a product operating budget and a response plan before broad promotion. Do not make on-demand AI inference part of every free completion.

Track contribution after support alongside infrastructure gross margin. Track service-pilot revenue separately. CAC payback should use actual monthly customer contribution, and LTV should not be extrapolated from a few weeks of retention.

## 8. Validation gates and experiments

All thresholds are internal starting hypotheses, not industry benchmarks or statistically powered conclusions.

| Gate | Evidence to collect | Continue / reconsider |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buyer demand | 12–15 interviews; at least 3 customers pay for a scoped pilot | If they only want a one-off design job, sell a service rather than assuming SaaS retention |
| Creator activation | 8–10 observed builds using a starter template | Target a median ≤15 minutes to a publishable short quiz; simplify authoring if users need founder assistance |
| Participant value | At least 300 non-test starts across 3 topics and 2 sources | Target ≥65% completion from starts and ≥20% second-quiz completion within 14 days among eligible opted-in measurement cohorts; inspect source/topic effects |
| Social acquisition | At least 500 eligible completions with referral measurement | Initial target ≥0.15 attributable new completions per completer; do not call it self-sustaining virality unless a defensible coefficient exceeds 1 |
| Trust | At least 10 observed disclosure tests and open feedback | ≥8/10 can explain who receives research data and that refusal is allowed; investigate every material misconception |
| Paid value | Pilot action logs and 30–60-day follow-up | At least 2 of the first 3 delivered pilots renew/commit to another campaign and identify an action influenced by the report |
| Economics | 4 weeks of actual delivery and support costs | Target ≥70% infrastructure/payment margin, with positive contribution after support at expected free-audience scale; adjust volume/pricing if not achieved |

Do not optimize research opt-in rate at the expense of comprehension. Do not impose a 20% consumer-to-paid conversion assumption: the buyer and participant are different populations.

If consumer retention is weak but businesses renew, position primarily as a branded quiz creation service/SaaS. If consumers return but businesses do not pay, test low-cost optional cosmetics and sponsorship only after scale, rather than inventing a data marketplace. If neither side works, revisit the value proposition before adding tiers.

## 9. Decisions before pricing is public

- Confirm the initial buyer niche, first launch markets, and adult research-pilot scope.
- Validate $29 creator and $99 business willingness to pay; choose one initial paid tier.
- Agree the free publication limit, existing-user grandfathering, and operational fair-use terms.
- Approve participant disclosures, private/shared result behavior, report thresholds, and retention rules.
- Decide a monthly operating budget and the support hours the founder can actually provide.

No billing, outreach, data collection changes, or pricing changes are implemented by this document.
